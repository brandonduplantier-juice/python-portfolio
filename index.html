Python - PyTorch - Bioinformatics - Early Research Write-up

Does Genomic AI Beat the Cheap Baselines?

A self-funded research project testing whether a large AI model trained on DNA can predict the measured effect of DNA changes better than older, cheaper scoring methods. The first experiment used a public lab dataset of 3,893 single-letter changes in the BRCA1 gene (Findlay et al., Nature 2018). I ran a 7-billion-parameter DNA model (Evo 2) on one home graphics card, kept the big model frozen, and trained a small prediction layer on top. Scoring held out one whole region of the gene at a time across 12 regions, and every method was compared on the same 3,593 variants. Result: the established scores performed better. I reported that and stopped, with no tuning until something won. Data checks matched the published figures, with zero reference mismatches when placing variants on the genome. Computer analysis on lab data only, not clinical, not peer reviewed. AI assistants were used for engineering and review under my direction. Code is private.

Biological Age and Mortality Dashboard

This project asks whether a body that appears older than its actual age is also at greater risk of dying. I tested that question using data from 28,510 adults in the United States who were followed by the government for as long as 19 years. The biological age estimate uses nine routine blood measurements available through a standard laboratory panel. Each additional year of biological age was associated with about a 4.4 percent higher mortality hazard (hazard ratio 1.044). Biological age also predicted survival better than chronological age alone. The dashboard lets users filter the population by age and sex and compare the survival curves directly.

DNA Methylation Aging Clock

This is the modeling work behind the interactive aging clock. The project starts with roughly 450,000 chemical markers measured along the DNA of 656 people. The model narrows those markers down to 176 that are most useful for predicting age. On a completely separate group of 689 people, the average error was 6.4 years; on the held out portion of the training data it was 5.7 years. The separate group is the result that matters, because that data played no role in building the model. As an additional check, many of the markers selected by the model overlap with markers used by an established published aging clock. That overlap suggests the model identified a meaningful biological signal instead of only fitting patterns that were unique to its training data.

Single Cell Aging Pathway Analysis

Aging does not affect every part of the body in the same way. It can even affect different cell types inside the same organ differently. This project examines mouse lung tissue one cell type at a time and compares age related changes across 11 cell types. The main methodological decision is just as important as the biological result. Individual cells from the same animal are not independent observations. Treating them as if they came from separate animals can make weak patterns appear statistically significant. This analysis groups the cells by animal and treats each animal as the unit of comparison, which avoids that mistake.

What the Readmissions Program Cannot See

Medicare penalizes hospitals when too many patients return within 30 days of being discharged. I analyzed the government's results file for 3,055 hospitals to see what the scoring system measures and what it leaves out. More than one third of the records have no score because a hospital needs at least 25 qualifying patients before Medicare will grade it. That rule removes many small and rural hospitals from the program's view. The system also compares hospitals with one another instead of using a fixed standard, which means roughly half of graded hospitals will remain on the wrong side of the line even if care improves across the country. Finally, a hospital that performs badly for one condition is only slightly more likely to perform badly for another. That raises questions about treating six loosely related conditions as one measure of overall hospital quality. The analysis was built with SQL and Python, and the full code is published with the project.

Hospital Readmission Analysis in Six Tools

A readmission happens when a patient returns to the hospital within 30 days of being discharged. Hospitals can be penalized when their readmission rates are too high. This project looks at which patients appear most at risk and answers the same question in six different tools. The dataset is a synthetic practice dataset containing 300 patients and was designed to resemble the structure of the Medicare program. The logistic regression compared readmission odds across diagnoses. Results come from a synthetic practice dataset and do not establish effects in real patients. Patients with a follow up appointment also appeared less likely to return, but the difference was not large enough to support a confident conclusion. I reported that uncertainty instead of presenting the pattern as a proven result.
