My current work, for a search and rescue organization covering a remote part of western Alaska with no roads in. Six tools, each answering one question a search leader actually asks. What the Readmissions Program Cannot See

Medicare penalizes hospitals when too many patients return within 30 days of being discharged. I analyzed the government's results file for 3,055 hospitals to see what the scoring system measures and what it leaves out. More than one third of the records have no score because a hospital needs at least 25 qualifying patients before Medicare will grade it. That rule removes many small and rural hospitals from the program's view. The system also compares hospitals with one another instead of using a fixed standard, which means roughly half of graded hospitals will remain on the wrong side of the line even if care improves across the country. Finally, a hospital that performs badly for one condition is only slightly more likely to perform badly for another. That raises questions about treating six loosely related conditions as one measure of overall hospital quality. The analysis was built with SQL and Python, and the full code is published with the project.

Hospital Readmission Analysis in Six Tools

A readmission happens when a patient returns to the hospital within 30 days of being discharged. Hospitals can be penalized when their readmission rates are too high. This project looks at which patients appear most at risk and answers the same question in six different tools. The dataset is a synthetic practice dataset containing 300 patients and was designed to resemble the structure of the Medicare program. Heart failure patients returned about 20 times more often than hip and knee replacement patients, who had the lowest risk. Patients with a follow up appointment also appeared less likely to return, but the difference was not large enough to support a confident conclusion. I reported that uncertainty instead of presenting the pattern as a proven result.

Interactive Biological Age Dashboard

The age of your body can differ from the number of years since you were born. This dashboard estimates biological age from a blood sample and shows which measurements are driving the prediction. Users can move a slider and watch the estimated age change in real time. On people the model had not seen during training, its predictions were off by about 5.7 years on average. On a completely separate group of 689 people, the error was 6.4 years. That second result matters more because it shows how the model performed on data that played no role in building it, so it is the result I lead with.

Biological Age and Mortality Dashboard

This project asks whether a body that appears older than its actual age is also at greater risk of dying. I tested that question using data from 28,510 adults in the United States who were followed by the government for as long as 19 years. The biological age estimate uses nine routine blood measurements available through a standard laboratory panel. Each additional year of biological age was associated with about a 4.5 percent increase in mortality risk. Biological age also predicted survival better than chronological age alone. The dashboard lets users filter the population by age and sex and compare the survival curves directly.

Longevity Intervention Evidence Explorer

Claims about supplements, diets, and fasting are everywhere, but the amount of attention an idea receives is not the same as the amount of evidence behind it. This tool grades longevity interventions using the same six criteria for every claim. It shows what has been tested in cells, animals, and humans, then compares the strength of that research with the intervention's popularity. Nothing receives the highest possible grade because that grade requires strong human research that follows people across an entire lifetime. No such study exists yet. Every score links back to the published research so users can review the evidence for themselves.

DNA Methylation Aging Clock

This is the modeling work behind the interactive aging clock. The project starts with roughly 450,000 chemical markers measured along the DNA of 656 people. The model narrows those markers down to 176 that are most useful for predicting age and reaches an average error of about 5.7 years on people it did not see during training. As an additional check, many of the markers selected by the model overlap with markers used by an established published aging clock. That overlap suggests the model identified a meaningful biological signal instead of only fitting patterns that were unique to its training data.

Single Cell Aging Pathway Analysis

Aging does not affect every part of the body in the same way. It can even affect different cell types inside the same organ differently. This project examines mouse lung tissue one cell type at a time and compares age related changes across 11 cell types. The main methodological decision is just as important as the biological result. Individual cells from the same animal are not independent observations. Treating them as if they came from separate animals can make weak patterns appear statistically significant. This analysis groups the cells by animal and treats each animal as the unit of comparison, which avoids that mistake.
