Project Write-up - Early Research - Bioinformatics

Does Genomic AI Beat the Cheap Baselines?

A self-funded research project comparing a large DNA model against established scoring methods on public lab data. Early stage, in progress. Computer analysis only.

Read this first

The first experiment in this project produced a negative result: the older, cheaper methods did better than the AI model. Nothing here is scientifically validated, peer reviewed, or clinical. What this page shows is how the experiment was set up and checked.

The question

What predictive information does genomic AI add beyond reliable, inexpensive biological annotations?

Put plainly: large AI models trained on DNA sequence are new and expensive to run. Older scoring methods are cheap and well established. Published work disagrees about whether the new models beat the old ones at predicting what a DNA change does. I wrote that disagreement down before running anything, along with a rule: a negative result is a finding, not a failure, and I do not tune until something wins.

The project is self-funded and runs on one home computer with a consumer graphics card, using free public data. First recorded work is dated October 17, 2026.

The first experiment

The data is a public lab dataset (Findlay et al., Nature 2018, MaveDB urn:mavedb:00000097-0-2) in which scientists measured the effect of nearly every single-letter change in key parts of the BRCA1 gene. These are lab measurements in cells. They say nothing about any person.

  • Each change was run through Evo 2, a 7-billion-parameter DNA model, and the model's internal numbers were kept. The big model was frozen, meaning it was not retrained.
  • A small, simple prediction layer was trained on top of those numbers.
  • Scoring left out one whole region of the gene at a time, so the layer was never scored on a region it had trained on.
  • The result was compared against established scores on exactly the same changes.
  • Uncertainty was estimated by resampling whole regions instead of individual changes, because neighbouring changes are not independent.

The result

The established scores did better than both versions of the AI model on this dataset.

The measure is rank correlation: how well a method's ordering of the changes agrees with the lab's ordering, where 0 is no agreement and 1 is perfect. The trained layer on Evo 2 scored 0.29 on held-out regions, and raw Evo 2 with no training scored 0.28. The best established score was clearly higher than both.

The trained layer was not shown to beat raw Evo 2. The difference between them was 0.0147, with an uncertainty range that crosses zero. Four other ways of summarising the model's numbers did worse than the raw score.

I reported that and stopped. This is a negative result about this particular design on this particular lab experiment, not about all genomic models.

Engineering problems solved

  • Reported a negative result and stopped instead of adjusting the method until something won. The limits of the conclusion were written down before and after.
  • Made it impossible for the answers to leak into a comparison. A public file needed for a planned comparison also contains the lab answers. The loader was redesigned so those columns are never read at all, and a test with planted marker values confirmed none got through.
  • Placed every change on the genome correctly for a gene that runs on the reverse strand and has 23 coding segments, which is a known source of quiet, plausible-looking errors.
  • Got a 7-billion-parameter model running on a single consumer graphics card after working out which versions cannot run on that hardware and resolving a chain of incompatible software versions.
  • Replaced a slow calculation with a faster equivalent and proved they agree to about 12 decimal places.

How results are checked

  • Every check must be shown to fail on bad input before its pass is trusted.
  • Tests are themselves tested, by deliberately breaking the code and confirming a test catches it.
  • Prepared data is cross-checked against an independent source. Agreeing with yourself is not validation.
  • Earlier results are never overwritten. Every file version gets a new name.
  • Decisions are logged in a record that is only ever added to. A changed decision is replaced by a new entry, never edited.
  • A second and third dataset are kept unopened so they can serve as a clean test later.

Limits

  • The headline result is negative.
  • One gene, one lab experiment, one model, one design.
  • No outside validation. The reserved datasets have not been opened.
  • Even later it will not be a perfectly clean test: the AI model saw BRCA1 sequence during its own original training. Only leakage of the lab answers can be controlled.
  • Nothing here is about aging, and nothing here is clinical. There is no patient data, no diagnosis, and no risk prediction.
  • Review so far is by AI assistants and by me. No human scientist has reviewed the work.
  • The comparison figures were recorded in late October 2026.

My role and how AI was used

This is a solo project and I am its principal investigator. I set the question, the rules of evidence, and the stopping rule, and I approve every significant step.

AI assistants were used for engineering and for review, under my direction. That is AI review. It is not independent validation and I do not describe it as such.
